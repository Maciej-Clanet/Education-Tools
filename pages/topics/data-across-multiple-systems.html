More explanation and examples

Potential benefit

Authorised staff in different departments can use current records. Remote access can reduce waiting and help staff collaborate.

Decisions to make

Who may view, edit or delete each kind of record? Does a user need access from home, a classroom or only a specialist office?

Dependency to consider

If the shared service is unavailable, several departments may lose access at once. Staff need an agreed fallback for essential work.

Better access means making the right information available to the right people—not letting everyone see everything.

More explanation and examples

Lower sensitivity · public timetable

A genuinely public course timetable is intended to be shared. Altering it could still send students to the wrong lesson.

Higher sensitivity · contact details

An exposed phone number or address affects privacy; an incorrect contact may prevent an important message reaching home.

Very sensitive · safeguarding or medical information

Unauthorised disclosure could expose a student to serious harm. Alteration or loss may prevent appropriate support.

The more sensitive the data, the greater the potential impact of unauthorised access, alteration or loss. Limit access to the information each role actually needs.

26 · Data across multiple systems

Finish by weighing the effects

Why the benefit matters

Correct registers are needed every lesson. Removing repeated checking can help staff every day.

Why the concern matters

An outage can interrupt several classes at once. That makes reliable access and a safe fallback important.

5 · Justified judgement

On balance, shared records are likely to help this college because accurate class lists and fewer repeated updates benefit daily teaching. These gains are likely to outweigh the setup effort if migration is tested, staff are trained and essential attendance work has a safe fallback.

If access remains unreliable, delaying the full rollout would be more sensible.

Decision + most important reason + conditions. Explain why those conditions matter; adding the word “if” alone is not evaluation.

An extended answer needs enough developed, relevant reasoning for the question. Five labels, five definitions or five sentences do not guarantee high marks.

Exam-style practice

Explain consequences for staff, students and the college. These are practice questions, not an official mark scheme.

Draft answers save automatically in this browser.

4 marks

Question 1

Explain two benefits of allowing authorised staff to access shared data from multiple systems.

Show answer guide
  • Explain one access benefit with its effect: teachers can use current attendance in classrooms, allowing quicker follow-up.
  • Explain a second distinct benefit: reception updates contacts once, reducing repeated entry and mistakes across departments.
  • For four marks, develop two benefit → applied impact links, rather than naming access and productivity.
4 marks

Question 2

Explain two implementation issues an organisation may face when introducing a shared information system.

Show answer guide
  • Migration may place data in the wrong field or omit records; this could put a student on the wrong register, so source comparisons and workflow tests are needed.
  • Staff may not know the new process, slowing attendance entry; training and launch support reduce disruption.
  • For four marks, explain two distinct issues and their consequences. Other applied issues, such as integration failures or badly timed changeover, can also be valid.
6 marks

Question 3

Discuss how using data across multiple systems could affect productivity in a college.

Show answer guide
  • Current contacts available across authorised systems reduce duplicate typing and speed up communication with families.
  • Reliable shared data can speed up reporting and leave more staff time for student support.
  • Poor integration, downtime or inadequate training can instead create checking, correction and delays across several departments.
  • Develop both benefits and concerns with college examples. Reach a supported judgement that depends on reliability, implementation and user support.
8 marks

Question 4

Analyse the security and access implications of allowing staff to use student data from multiple devices and locations.

Show answer guide
  • Remote and classroom access let authorised staff respond sooner, but access should match the task: attendance access does not justify safeguarding access.
  • More devices and connections create more possible routes for misuse. Link authentication, permissions and protected devices to limiting that exposure.
  • Consistent permissions and logs may improve oversight; a compromised account with excessive access could affect many records.
  • Explain harm to individuals: exposed contact or safeguarding information can affect privacy or safety; altered records can misdirect support.
  • Connect wider access to the protection it needs. Weigh convenience against sensitivity and justify appropriate restrictions rather than simply banning or allowing all remote access.
12 marks

Question 5

A college plans to replace several separate systems with one connected student-information platform. Evaluate the likely impact on the organisation.

Show answer guide
  • Access and productivity: current course/contact records could reduce duplicate entry and speed up registers, enquiries and reports. Explain the effect on staff and students.
  • Cost: compare migration, training and setup with continuing licences/support and possible savings in staff time. Benefits may take time to repay initial costs.
  • Implementation: migrating Alex’s records incorrectly or launching during registration could disrupt several teams. Testing, training and a phased changeover need time and resources.
  • Security: consistent permissions may improve control, but wider access and sensitive records require protection. Explain how limiting access affects convenience and risk.
  • Connect factors: paying for training may temporarily reduce productivity but prevent recurring errors; funding protection can make remote access more appropriate.
  • For higher marks, weigh the important impacts and reach a conditional recommendation grounded in the college’s needs. Explain what would change the judgement, such as unreliable integration or unaffordable support. A five-factor list alone is insufficient.

Glossary

Quick glossary

Centralised data
Data whose main records are maintained in a common shared store.
Synchronisation
Keeping copies in step by exchanging updates.
Shared data
Information made available to relevant authorised systems and users.
Migration
Moving existing records into a new system.
Trade-off
A gain in one area alongside a cost or difficulty in another.
Implementation
The process of putting a system into use successfully.
Integration
Making separate systems work together.
Access control
Rules about who can reach or change data.
Productivity
How effectively users can complete their work.

Remember

Weigh both sides

  • Shared systems can improve access and productivity.
  • They can also increase cost, complexity, and security risk.
  • Strong answers balance benefits against concerns.
