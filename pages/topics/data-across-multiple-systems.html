Should every department maintain its own separate copy?

Different people and systems need relevant parts of the same information.

More explanation and examples

At our example college, different people need student information for different jobs.

Teachers · classroom PCs

Record attendance and check who needs support.

Reception · student records

Update enrolment and contact information.

Managers · reporting

Compare attendance patterns across courses.

Finance/admin · fee records

Use relevant student details for administration.

Remote staff · authorised devices

Prepare work or follow up enquiries away from the office.

Should every department keep completely separate copies of the same student information?

Several people may need the same facts, from different locations. Specialist systems can use shared data for their own tasks and feed reports.

Connected systems can make current information available where authorised users need it.

More explanation and examples

Potential benefit

Authorised staff in different departments can use current records. Remote access can reduce waiting and help staff collaborate.

Decisions to make

Who may view, edit or delete each kind of record? Does a user need access from home, a classroom or only a specialist office?

Dependency to consider

If the shared service is unavailable, several departments may lose access at once. Staff need an agreed fallback for essential work.

Better access means making the right information available to the right people—not letting everyone see everything.

  1. Test a migrated sample

More explanation and examples

Fields are mapped incorrectly

Dates of birth appear in the wrong field.
Reduce the risk: validate formats and compare migrated samples with source records.

Records are missing

Some students disappear from the new system.
Reduce the risk: reconcile record counts and check which records moved.

Systems disagree

Attendance updates fail to reach reports.
Reduce the risk: test complete workflows and update behaviour.

Staff are unprepared

Teachers cannot enter attendance confidently.
Reduce the risk: practise real tasks and provide launch support.

Changeover interrupts lessons

The new system is unavailable during registration.
Reduce the risk: trial a phased rollout, choose a suitable time and prepare a fallback and protected copy of the original data.

A value can pass validation yet still be wrong. Compare migrated data with its source; do not rely on format checks alone.

  2. Teacher updates another copy
  3. Finance updates again

  1. Reception updates once
  2. Authorised systems receive the current details

Potential gain

Less repeated entry and fewer inconsistencies free staff for student support. Automated reporting can reduce preparation time.

Possible setback

If systems disagree, staff spend time checking and correcting records. An outage or confusing interface can interrupt several jobs.

Connected systems improve productivity only if sharing is reliable and the system is well implemented. Initial training can slow work before longer-term gains appear.

15 · Data across multiple systems

Why sensitivity matters

  1. PUBLICCourse timetable
  2. PERSONALPhone / address
  3. HIGHLY SENSITIVESafeguarding / medical information

↓ Increasing potential harm from disclosure, alteration or loss

Why does the impact change?

Exposed contacts affect privacy; exposed safeguarding information could put Alex at serious risk. Changed or missing information can prevent support.

Public does not mean unprotected: altering a timetable can still send students to the wrong lesson.

More explanation and examples

Lower sensitivity · public timetable

A genuinely public course timetable is intended to be shared. Altering it could still send students to the wrong lesson.

Higher sensitivity · contact details

An exposed phone number or address affects privacy; an incorrect contact may prevent an important message reaching home.

Very sensitive · safeguarding or medical information

Unauthorised disclosure could expose a student to serious harm. Alteration or loss may prevent appropriate support.

The more sensitive the data, the greater the potential impact of unauthorised access, alteration or loss. Limit access to the information each role actually needs.

4 · Conditional judgement
The change is likely to improve access and productivity if migration is reliable, access is controlled, and staff training and support are properly funded.

The judgement depends on conditions.

More explanation and examples

The college wants to replace separate spreadsheets and systems with a shared student-information platform.

Access

Teachers need class attendance; reception needs editable contacts; managers need reports. Safeguarding access is restricted.

Cost

Budget for migration and training as well as licences and ongoing support.

Implementation

Check Alex’s course/contact updates migrate correctly; test an attendance-to-report workflow before launch.

Productivity

Entering contact details once can reduce repeated work and help staff reach families sooner.

Security

Apply appropriate permissions and protect remote devices so convenience does not expose sensitive records.

The shared platform is likely to improve access and productivity because staff can use current records instead of checking separate copies. However, migration errors could disrupt registers, and wider access requires protection. Overall, the benefits are likely to outweigh the drawbacks if the college funds ongoing support, tests migration and trains staff before rollout.

The judgement is conditional: it explains what must go well for the benefits to be realised.

How to write a balanced answer

Scenario: the college replaces separate copies with shared records.

  1. BENEFIT Shared records improve access…
  2. IMPACT

    …so staff can use current details without waiting for another department.

  3. TRADE-OFF

    However, unreliable migration could put the wrong course on a register.

  4. APPLICATION

    For Alex, this could mean appearing on the wrong class list.

  5. JUDGEMENT

    Overall, the change is worthwhile if migration is tested and staff are trained.

“Overall, it is good” needs a reason and a condition.

More explanation and examples
  1. 1 · Identify a benefit
    Shared records improve access because…
  2. 2 · Explain the impact
    This means staff can…
  3. 3 · Add a trade-off
    However…
  4. 4 · Apply it
    This matters in the college because…
  5. 5 · Reach a judgement
    Overall… if…

Weak conclusion

“Overall, it is good.”
This gives no reason or condition.

Stronger conclusion

“Overall, the shared system is likely to improve productivity if permissions, training and migration are managed effectively.”
This identifies what the outcome depends on.

Weigh the most important effects for the scenario. Five definitions without applied explanations do not make a balanced judgement.

Exam-style practice

Explain consequences for staff, students and the college. These are practice questions, not an official mark scheme.

Draft answers save automatically in this browser.

4 marks

Question 1

Explain two benefits of allowing authorised staff to access shared data from multiple systems.

Show answer guide
  • Explain one access benefit with its effect: teachers can use current attendance in classrooms, allowing quicker follow-up.
  • Explain a second distinct benefit: reception updates contacts once, reducing repeated entry and mistakes across departments.
  • For four marks, develop two benefit → applied impact links, rather than naming access and productivity.
4 marks

Question 2

Explain two implementation issues an organisation may face when introducing a shared information system.

Show answer guide
  • Migration may place data in the wrong field or omit records; this could put a student on the wrong register, so source comparisons and workflow tests are needed.
  • Staff may not know the new process, slowing attendance entry; training and launch support reduce disruption.
  • For four marks, explain two distinct issues and their consequences. Other applied issues, such as integration failures or badly timed changeover, can also be valid.
6 marks

Question 3

Discuss how using data across multiple systems could affect productivity in a college.

Show answer guide
  • Current contacts available across authorised systems reduce duplicate typing and speed up communication with families.
  • Reliable shared data can speed up reporting and leave more staff time for student support.
  • Poor integration, downtime or inadequate training can instead create checking, correction and delays across several departments.
  • Develop both benefits and concerns with college examples. Reach a supported judgement that depends on reliability, implementation and user support.
8 marks

Question 4

Analyse the security and access implications of allowing staff to use student data from multiple devices and locations.

Show answer guide
  • Remote and classroom access let authorised staff respond sooner, but access should match the task: attendance access does not justify safeguarding access.
  • More devices and connections create more possible routes for misuse. Link authentication, permissions and protected devices to limiting that exposure.
  • Consistent permissions and logs may improve oversight; a compromised account with excessive access could affect many records.
  • Explain harm to individuals: exposed contact or safeguarding information can affect privacy or safety; altered records can misdirect support.
  • Connect wider access to the protection it needs. Weigh convenience against sensitivity and justify appropriate restrictions rather than simply banning or allowing all remote access.
12 marks

Question 5

A college plans to replace several separate systems with one connected student-information platform. Evaluate the likely impact on the organisation.

Show answer guide
  • Access and productivity: current course/contact records could reduce duplicate entry and speed up registers, enquiries and reports. Explain the effect on staff and students.
  • Cost: compare migration, training and setup with continuing licences/support and possible savings in staff time. Benefits may take time to repay initial costs.
  • Implementation: migrating Alex’s records incorrectly or launching during registration could disrupt several teams. Testing, training and a phased changeover need time and resources.
  • Security: consistent permissions may improve control, but wider access and sensitive records require protection. Explain how limiting access affects convenience and risk.
  • Connect factors: paying for training may temporarily reduce productivity but prevent recurring errors; funding protection can make remote access more appropriate.
  • For higher marks, weigh the important impacts and reach a conditional recommendation grounded in the college’s needs. Explain what would change the judgement, such as unreliable integration or unaffordable support. A five-factor list alone is insufficient.

Glossary

Quick glossary

Shared data
Information made available to relevant authorised systems and users.
Migration
Moving existing records into a new system.
Trade-off
A gain in one area alongside a cost or difficulty in another.
Implementation
The process of putting a system into use successfully.
Integration
Making separate systems work together.
Access control
Rules about who can reach or change data.
Productivity
How effectively users can complete their work.

Remember

Weigh both sides

  • Shared systems can improve access and productivity.
  • They can also increase cost, complexity, and security risk.
  • Strong answers balance benefits against concerns.
